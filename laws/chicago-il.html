Chicago chicken laws

City of Chicago allows backyard laying hens without a numeric flock cap or residential chicken permit, but strictly prohibits keeping or slaughtering any animal for meat and enforces strict sanitation, rodent-control, and noise rules.

Last checked 2026-10-25 against official city sources. Rules change, so confirm with the city before buying birds.

Quick facts

Chicken limit
No numeric limit in city code for egg-laying hens (must meet sanitation, noise, and space standards)
Roosters
Not explicitly banned by species, but crowing that disturbs neighbors violates Chicago noise and animal nuisance ordinances
Permit or license
No residential permit required for keeping hens for eggs (Illinois Dept. of Agriculture livestock premises registration recommended; live-poultry retail/market facilities require a license under Sec. 7-40-135)
Last checked
2026-10-25

This applies inside Chicago city limits. ZIP codes and mailing addresses can cross city lines, and nearby towns, villages, and counties have their own rules. HOA rules and leases can be stricter.

Before you apply

  • Check property deed restrictions, HOA bylaws, or landlord lease terms before acquiring birds.
  • Confirm your coop design meets Chicago building and accessory-structure setback rules and rodent-proofing standards.

Key rules

  • Under Chicago Municipal Code Section 7-12-300, no person may own, keep, or slaughter any animal (including poultry) intended to be used for food/meat purposes; animals kept solely for eggs or milk are exempt from the possession ban, but on-site slaughtering is strictly prohibited ($50 to $1,000 fine).
  • Coops and enclosures must be kept clean, sanitary, and rodent-proof so they do not attract rats or create odor or noise nuisances under Chapter 7-12 and Chapter 7-28.
  • Birds may not run at large off the owner's premises.
