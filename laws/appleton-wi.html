Appleton chicken laws

City of Appleton allows up to 6 hens on single-family or two-family properties with an annual Health Department permit and a pre-license inspection. Roosters are not allowed.

Last checked 2026-10-18 against official city sources. Rules change, so confirm with the city before buying birds.

Quick facts

Chicken limit
Up to 6 chickens (hens only)
Roosters
Roosters not allowed (the rules define chickens as hens only)
Permit or license
Annual Health Department permit; the 2026 application lists an $81 pre-inspection fee and a $28 annual license
Last checked
2026-10-18

This applies inside Appleton city limits. ZIP codes and mailing addresses can cross city lines, and nearby towns, villages, and counties have their own rules. HOA rules and leases can be stricter.

Before you apply

  • Read the city's Chicken Keeping Rules and Regulations.
  • Register your premises with the Wisconsin Livestock Identification Consortium (free).
  • Submit the application with a diagram of the coop location and distances to neighboring structures, a coop design and materials plan, and a manure disposal plan.
  • Schedule the pre-licensing inspection. Renters need written permission from the property owner.

Key rules

  • Single-family or two-family dwellings only; not commercial or mixed-use properties.
  • Coop must be in the rear yard, at least 3 feet from side and rear lot lines, 10 feet from other structures on the parcel unless attached to a garage or shed, and 25 feet from a home, church, school, or business on an abutting lot.
  • Coop floor must give at least 2 square feet per bird, be a hard cleanable surface (no dirt floor), and the coop cannot be taller than 8 feet. The attached run is limited to 100 square feet.
  • Chickens must stay in the coop or attached run at all times. Off-site sale of eggs is prohibited.
  • Coops of 50 square feet or more need a building permit; electricity needs an electrical permit.
  • Unusual illness or death must be reported to the Health Department immediately.
