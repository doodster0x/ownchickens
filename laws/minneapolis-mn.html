Local laws · Minnesota

Minneapolis chicken laws

City of Minneapolis allows backyard chickens and other fowl with an annual fowl permit. You need neighbor consent, a fowl education course, zoning approval, and an inspection before getting birds.

Last checked 2026-10-18 against official city sources. Rules change, so confirm with the city before buying birds.

Quick facts

Chicken limit
Permit fee tiers cover 1-6, 7-15, and 16-30 birds
Roosters
Separate rooster permit fee ($110); neighbor consent required
Permit or license
Annual fowl permit: $32 for 1-6 birds, $58 for 7-15, $84 for 16-30 (application and renewal)
Last checked
2026-10-18

This applies inside Minneapolis city limits. ZIP codes and mailing addresses can cross city lines, and nearby towns, villages, and counties have their own rules. HOA rules and leases can be stricter.

Before you apply

  • Do not buy any birds until the permit is approved.
  • Contact 311 to apply. Show proof of consent from your neighbors.
  • Attend an approved fowl education course.
  • Get zoning approval from the Community Planning and Economic Development department.
  • Pass an inspection by an Animal Care and Control officer.

Key rules

  • The permit covers chickens, turkeys, ducks, pigeons, and quail.
  • Permits renew every year.
  • Full rules are in Minneapolis Code of Ordinances section 63.90, Fowl.
