Chicken coop buying guide

Prefab, kit, DIY, or shed conversion? Here is how to judge any coop on space, predator-proofing, ventilation, and cleaning, before you spend money.

The five things that actually matter

  1. Real floor space. About 4 square feet per standard hen inside, plus about 10 square feet per hen in the run. Measure it yourself; capacity claims on listings are often optimistic.
  2. Predator resistance. 1/2-inch hardware cloth on windows, vents, and the run; solid doors; latches that need two steps to open.
  3. Ventilation. Vents high on the walls, above the roost, covered with hardware cloth.
  4. Cleaning access. A large door or removable tray you can reach every corner through. Small hatches make cleaning miserable.
  5. Weather durability. A roof that sheds water, a floor off wet ground, and materials that hold up to your winters.

Upgrades most kit coops need

  • Replace or cover chicken wire with 1/2-inch hardware cloth, attached with screws and washers.
  • Add a carabiner or second latch to every door.
  • Add a dig apron around the run.
  • Seal or paint exposed wood, and let it dry fully before birds move in.
  • Set the coop on pavers or blocks so the floor stays dry.
  • Add vents if the coop smells of ammonia or has condensation in cold weather.

Before you buy: a quick checklist

  • Does the size fit your local rules? Some cities cap coop square footage or height, or require a building permit over a certain size. Check local laws.
  • Will it fit inside the required setbacks on your lot?
  • Can you reach the nest boxes, feeder, and water without crawling in?
  • Is there room for a heated waterer and an outdoor-rated power source if your winters freeze?
  • What does the return policy say if it arrives damaged?

Frequently asked questions

How big a coop do I need for 4 chickens?

About 16 square feet of coop floor (about 4 square feet per standard hen) and about 40 square feet of run. Larger breeds need more.

Are prefab chicken coops worth it?

They can be for 2 to 4 hens if you measure the real floor space and upgrade weak spots like chicken wire and simple latches. Heavier-duty or DIY coops usually last longer.

Do I need a permit to build a chicken coop?

Some cities require a building permit for coops over a certain size, and many have setback and height limits. Check your city rules before you buy or build.

What should a coop floor be made of?

A solid, cleanable surface such as sealed plywood, vinyl over plywood, or concrete. Dirt floors are hard to keep dry and some cities do not allow them.
