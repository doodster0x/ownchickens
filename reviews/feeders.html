What a good feeder does

  • Holds enough that you are not refilling daily. For a small flock, many keepers like a feeder that lasts about a week or more.
  • Cuts waste. Hens flick feed out of open trays. Lips, ports, and narrow openings help, as do pellets instead of crumbles.
  • Keeps feed dry. Wet feed molds. Covered feeders or feeders kept under the coop or a run roof are best.
  • Discourages rodents and wild birds. Spilled feed attracts both, and wild birds can carry disease.
  • Is easy to clean. Feed cakes and molds in corners. You should be able to empty and scrub it.

Tips that matter more than the brand

  • Set the feeder lip at about the height of the hens' backs.
  • Fill it about half to two-thirds full to cut waste.
  • Clean it out between bags, and toss any clumped or moldy feed.
  • Keep a separate small dish for oyster shell and grit.
  • Bring feeders in or close them at night if rats or mice show up.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of chicken feeder wastes the least feed?

Port feeders and treadle feeders with pellets usually waste less than open trays or crumbles. Setting the feeder at back height and filling it partway also helps.

How big should a chicken feeder be?

Big enough that you are not refilling it every day. Many small-flock keepers aim for about a week of feed, kept dry under cover.

Do treadle feeders keep rats out?

They help, because the lid only opens when a bird steps on the plate. Clean up spills and store feed in a sealed bin too.
