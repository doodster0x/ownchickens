Keeping chickens cool in summer heat

Chickens handle cold better than heat. On hot, humid days, shade, airflow, and cool water keep your flock comfortable and safe.

Signs a hen is too hot

  • Panting with an open beak.
  • Wings held out away from the body.
  • Sitting still, lethargic, or not eating.
  • Pale comb and wattles.
  • Fewer eggs, or thinner shells.

Some panting on a warm afternoon is normal. A hen that is stumbling, unresponsive, or lying down needs help right away.

How to keep the flock cool

  • Shade. Make sure part of the run is shaded all afternoon. A tarp or shade cloth works.
  • Airflow. Open vents and windows, covered with hardware cloth. A coop that feels stuffy at night is too hot.
  • Cool water, and plenty of it. Add a second waterer in the shade and refill with cool water in the afternoon. Some keepers add poultry electrolytes during heat waves; follow the label.
  • Frozen treats in moderation. Chilled watermelon, cucumber, or a block of ice in a shallow pan.
  • A shallow damp area. Some hens like standing in a wet, shaded patch of soil.
  • Go easy on scratch grains in the heat; layer feed should still be most of the diet.
  • Do chores early. Avoid chasing or handling birds in the afternoon.

If a hen overheats

Move her to a shady, cool spot and offer cool (not ice-cold) water. Wetting her legs and feet with cool water can help. Do not dunk her whole body. Call a vet who treats poultry if she does not improve quickly.

Plan ahead for next summer

If your summers are hot, choose breeds that handle heat well. The breed comparison table rates heat tolerance, and the housing guide covers coop ventilation.

Frequently asked questions

How hot is too hot for chickens?

Chickens start to struggle as temperatures climb, especially with high humidity. Watch for panting, wings held out, and lethargy, and add shade, airflow, and cool water early.

Should I give my chickens ice water?

Cool water is good, and a block of ice in a pan or frozen fruit can help. For an overheated hen, use cool, not ice-cold, water.

Do chickens need electrolytes in hot weather?

Some keepers add poultry electrolytes during heat waves. They are optional; follow the label and keep plain water available too.
